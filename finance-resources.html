Worked Examples

Example 1 · Liquidity

Current Ratio

A company has current assets of ₹6,00,000 and current liabilities of ₹3,00,000.

Current Ratio = 6,00,000 ÷ 3,00,000
View solution

Current ratio = 2:1.

The company has ₹2 in current assets for every ₹1 of current liabilities. This ratio alone does not establish whether its liquidity is adequate.

Example 2 · Profitability

Net Profit Margin

A business earns revenue of ₹20,00,000 and net profit of ₹2,00,000.

Margin = (Net Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100
View solution

Margin = (₹2,00,000 ÷ ₹20,00,000) × 100 = 10%.

The business earns ₹10 in net profit for every ₹100 of revenue.

Example 3 · Cost Accounting

Break-Even Point

Fixed costs are ₹1,00,000, selling price is ₹50 per unit and variable cost is ₹30 per unit.

Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution per Unit
View solution

Contribution per unit = ₹50 − ₹30 = ₹20.

Break-even point = ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹20 = 5,000 units.

At 5,000 units, total revenue equals total costs under these assumptions.

Example 4 · Working Capital

Net Working Capital

Current assets are ₹12 lakh and current liabilities are ₹8 lakh.

Net Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities
View solution

Net working capital = ₹12 lakh − ₹8 lakh = ₹4 lakh.

A positive balance indicates current assets exceed current liabilities, but the composition and timing of cash flows also matter.

Self-Practice Questions

1. Current ratio calculation

Current assets are ₹9 lakh and current liabilities are ₹6 lakh. Calculate the current ratio.

Answer: ₹9 lakh ÷ ₹6 lakh = 1.5:1.

2. Contribution calculation

Selling price is ₹200 per unit and variable cost is ₹120 per unit. Find contribution per unit.

Answer: ₹200 − ₹120 = ₹80 per unit.

3. Simple interest calculation

Calculate simple interest on ₹50,000 at 8% per annum for 2 years.

Answer: ₹50,000 × 8 × 2 ÷ 100 = ₹8,000.

4. Break-even calculation

Fixed costs are ₹60,000 and contribution per unit is ₹15. Find the break-even volume.

Answer: ₹60,000 ÷ ₹15 = 4,000 units.
