Budgeting

Budgeting translates organisational plans into quantified financial and operational targets for a defined period.

1. Concept and meaning

Budgeting translates organisational plans into quantified financial and operational targets for a defined period.

Core idea: Budgets support coordination and control but should be realistic, updated when assumptions change and not encourage dysfunctional behaviour.

2. Key concepts and tools

Flexible Budget = budget recalculated for the actual activity level using fixed and variable cost behaviour. Cash budget estimates receipts, payments and financing needs.

Use the relevant formula or analytical framework only when its assumptions fit the problem. State units, time period and assumptions clearly.

3. Worked example / practical illustration

If variable cost is ₹30 per unit and output is 1,000 units, budgeted variable cost is ₹30,000, before fixed costs.

4. Classroom case study

A college event team has a ₹2 lakh budget. Students prepare a budget for venue, travel, publicity and contingency, then compare actual spending with plan.

Discussion prompts

  • What information is missing before making a recommendation?
  • What are the financial and non-financial risks?
  • Which assumptions should be tested?

5. Learning activity

BBA: BBA: prepare functional and cash budgets.

MBA: MBA: evaluate rolling budgets, zero-based budgeting, variance analysis and behavioural issues.
