Social

The social dimension of ESG examines how an organisation treats employees, customers, suppliers and communities, including safety, inclusion and human rights.

1. Concept and meaning

The social dimension of ESG examines how an organisation treats employees, customers, suppliers and communities, including safety, inclusion and human rights.

Core idea: Social performance includes policies and actual outcomes; a policy statement alone does not demonstrate positive impact.

2. Key concepts and tools

Indicators may include injury rates, employee turnover, training hours, diversity measures, customer complaints and supply-chain due-diligence findings.

Use the relevant formula or analytical framework only when its assumptions fit the problem. State units, time period and assumptions clearly.

3. Worked example / practical illustration

A company tracks workplace injuries per 200,000 hours worked to compare safety performance across periods, while explaining methodology and coverage.

4. Classroom case study

A supplier is accused of unsafe working conditions. Students propose a due-diligence, remediation and monitoring response.

Discussion prompts

  • What information is missing before making a recommendation?
  • What are the financial and non-financial risks?
  • Which assumptions should be tested?

5. Learning activity

BBA: BBA: map social issues to stakeholders.

MBA: MBA: assess human-rights due diligence, workforce metrics and supply-chain risk.

7. Key takeaway

Social performance includes policies and actual outcomes; a policy statement alone does not demonstrate positive impact.
