IPO

An initial public offering is a company's first offer of shares to public investors, subject to applicable regulatory and exchange requirements.

1. Concept and meaning

An initial public offering is a company's first offer of shares to public investors, subject to applicable regulatory and exchange requirements.

Core idea: A fresh issue raises funds for the company; an offer for sale transfers existing shares from selling shareholders. The two have different implications for company proceeds.

2. Key concepts and tools

Issue proceeds depend on shares issued and issue price. For an offer with a price band, bids are collected within the stated band under the offer process; allotment follows the applicable basis.

Use the relevant formula or analytical framework only when its assumptions fit the problem. State units, time period and assumptions clearly.

3. Worked example / practical illustration

If a company issues 1 crore fresh shares at ₹100 each, gross fresh-issue proceeds are ₹100 crore before issue expenses.

4. Classroom case study

Students review a sample offer document and distinguish fresh issue from offer for sale, identify use of proceeds and list investor risk factors.

Discussion prompts

  • What information is missing before making a recommendation?
  • What are the financial and non-financial risks?
  • Which assumptions should be tested?

5. Learning activity

BBA: BBA: map the IPO process from offer document to listing.

MBA: MBA: examine valuation, dilution, use of proceeds, risk factors and post-listing performance.
