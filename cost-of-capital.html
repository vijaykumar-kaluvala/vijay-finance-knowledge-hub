Cost of Capital

Cost of capital is the required return expected by providers of finance and is used as a benchmark for investment decisions.

1. Concept and meaning

Cost of capital is the required return expected by providers of finance and is used as a benchmark for investment decisions.

Core idea: Debt, preference shares, equity and retained earnings have different costs and risks; WACC reflects the weighted financing mix.

2. Key concepts and tools

After-tax cost of debt = Kd(1−T), where Kd is pre-tax debt cost and T is the applicable tax rate. WACC = Σ(weight of source × component cost), using market-value weights where appropriate.

Use the relevant formula or analytical framework only when its assumptions fit the problem. State units, time period and assumptions clearly.

3. Worked example / practical illustration

If pre-tax debt costs 10% and the assumed tax rate is 25%, the illustrative after-tax debt cost is 7.5%, provided the interest tax shield is available.

4. Classroom case study

A firm is considering a project with risk different from its existing business. Discuss whether the company-wide WACC is an appropriate discount rate.

Discussion prompts

  • What information is missing before making a recommendation?
  • What are the financial and non-financial risks?
  • Which assumptions should be tested?

5. Learning activity

BBA: BBA: calculate component costs and a simple WACC.

MBA: MBA: estimate cost of equity, choose weights and discuss project-specific discount rates.

7. Key takeaway

Debt, preference shares, equity and retained earnings have different costs and risks; WACC reflects the weighted financing mix.

Academic note: This is a learning resource, not investment advice. Verify current rules, market data and source documents before citing or making decisions.
