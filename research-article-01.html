FinTech-Driven Market Efficiency and Arbitrage Strategies in Indian Capital Markets

An Empirical Research Framework with Reference to the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)

Finance Research | Indian Capital Markets | Algorithmic Trading | Market Efficiency

Research status: Proposed empirical research framework. This page presents a research proposal and methodology, not completed empirical findings. Statistical results and conclusions must be added only after data collection and analysis.
Research domainFinancial Markets and FinTech
Geographical scopeIndian Capital Markets
ExchangesNSE and BSE
Proposed study periodJanuary 2021 – December 2025

Abstract

Financial technology has transformed the functioning of modern capital markets through algorithmic trading, electronic trading platforms, high-frequency trading, and data-driven investment systems. These developments have the potential to improve price discovery, liquidity and trading efficiency. However, technology-driven trading may also create new challenges relating to market volatility, execution costs, information asymmetry and short-lived arbitrage opportunities.

This proposed study examines the relationship between FinTech adoption and market efficiency in Indian capital markets, with particular reference to the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). It proposes an empirical examination of price discrepancies, trading volume, liquidity, volatility and potential arbitrage opportunities. The study intends to use quantitative secondary data and appropriate statistical techniques to assess whether technology-enabled trading contributes to more efficient price adjustments after considering transaction costs and execution constraints.

Keywords: FinTech, Market Efficiency, Arbitrage, Algorithmic Trading, High-Frequency Trading, NSE, BSE, Price Discovery.

1. Introduction

Financial markets play an important role in mobilising savings, allocating capital and supporting economic development. The rapid growth of financial technology has changed how securities are traded, how information is processed and how investment decisions are made.

Algorithmic trading and electronic order-matching systems enable market participants to execute transactions at high speed. These technologies may reduce delays in incorporating information into security prices. At the same time, differences in trading systems, liquidity and execution conditions may create temporary price discrepancies across markets.

The Indian securities market provides a relevant setting for examining these issues because NSE and BSE operate as major trading venues with electronic trading infrastructure. This research framework proposes to investigate cross-exchange price differences and the extent to which such differences represent economically actionable arbitrage opportunities.

2. Review of Literature

2.1 High-frequency trading and price discovery

Brogaard, Hendershott and Riordan (2014) examined the relationship between high-frequency trading and price discovery. Their work provides a foundation for understanding how technology-enabled trading can contribute to the incorporation of information into prices.

2.2 Algorithmic trading in Indian markets

Jawed and Chakrabarti (2018) studied algorithmic and co-location trading in India, focusing on the speed of information adjustments. This research is relevant to understanding technology-driven trading in the Indian market environment.

2.3 Trading performance and market efficiency

Syamala and Wadhwa (2020) investigated trading performance and market efficiency in the context of algorithmic trading. Their work provides a relevant basis for examining the relationship between trading technology and market outcomes.

2.4 Intraday trading activity

Sampath and Gopalaswamy (2020) examined intraday variability and trading volume in the National Stock Exchange. Their study is relevant to the analysis of intraday market behaviour and trading activity.

Note: This is a preliminary literature overview. The final research paper should include a systematic literature search, verified bibliographic details and a critical comparison of findings.

3. Research Gap

The proposed study will investigate the relationship between FinTech-enabled trading and market efficiency by focusing on cross-exchange price differences between NSE and BSE. It will examine whether observed price discrepancies remain economically meaningful after accounting for transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, liquidity and execution constraints.

The precise research gap must be established through a systematic review of existing studies, including recent Indian evidence. The study should not assume that cross-exchange arbitrage has been unexplored without verifying prior research.

4. Research Objectives

  1. To examine the relationship between FinTech-enabled trading and market efficiency in Indian capital markets.
  2. To analyse price discrepancies in selected securities listed on both NSE and BSE.
  3. To examine the relationship between trading volume, liquidity and price adjustments.
  4. To evaluate potential arbitrage opportunities after considering transaction costs and execution constraints.
  5. To assess the implications of technology-driven trading for market participants and regulators.

5. Research Questions

  1. Does FinTech-enabled trading contribute to improved market efficiency?
  2. What patterns of price discrepancies are observed between NSE and BSE?
  3. How are liquidity and trading volume associated with price adjustments?
  4. Do observed price differences represent viable arbitrage opportunities after transaction costs?
  5. What are the implications of algorithmic trading for Indian capital markets?

6. Research Hypotheses

H01FinTech-enabled trading has no significant relationship with market efficiency.

H03Liquidity has no significant relationship with cross-exchange price discrepancies.
H04Observed price discrepancies do not generate significant risk-adjusted arbitrage returns after transaction costs.

7. Research Methodology

7.1 Research design

The proposed study adopts a quantitative, analytical research design using secondary market data.

7.2 Study population and sample

The population will consist of securities listed on NSE and BSE. A proposed sample of 20–30 actively traded companies listed on both exchanges may be selected, subject to data availability, liquidity and consistency of trading records. The final sampling procedure and sample size will be justified in the completed study.

7.3 Study period

The proposed observation period is January 2021 to December 2025. The final period will depend on the availability, quality and comparability of the required data.

7.4 Sources of data

  • NSE official historical market data.
  • BSE official historical market data.
  • Exchange-published security prices and trading volumes.
  • Published research articles and academic databases.
  • Other verified market-data sources, where permitted and appropriately documented.

7.5 Proposed variables

VariableProposed measurement
Market efficiencyPrice adjustment behaviour and selected efficiency indicators
Price discrepancyDifference between comparable NSE and BSE prices at aligned timestamps
Trading volumeNumber or value of shares traded
LiquidityBid-ask spread and other available liquidity measures
VolatilityReturn variability over a defined interval
Arbitrage returnPotential return after estimated trading costs
Execution delayTime difference between signal and feasible execution, if reliable data are available

7.6 Proposed analytical techniques

  • Descriptive statistics.
  • Correlation analysis.
  • Regression analysis, subject to variable properties and model assumptions.
  • Time-series analysis and stationarity testing, where appropriate.
  • ARCH/GARCH models if volatility characteristics justify their use.
  • Robustness checks and sensitivity analysis.

7.7 Illustrative price discrepancy measure

Price Difference (%)
= [(NSE Price − BSE Price) / BSE Price] × 100

Prices must be aligned by timestamp and adjusted for relevant corporate actions. A positive or negative difference alone does not establish a profitable arbitrage opportunity.

7.8 Illustrative net arbitrage return

Net Arbitrage Return
= Gross Trading Gain − Transaction Costs − Taxes − Other Execution Costs

The final calculation should account for brokerage, exchange charges, securities transaction tax, applicable taxes, bid-ask spreads, slippage, market impact and the feasibility of executing both legs of a trade.

8. Scope of the Study

  • Indian equity capital markets.
  • Selected securities traded on NSE and BSE.
  • Technology-enabled trading and market efficiency.
  • Cross-exchange price discrepancies and potential arbitrage.
  • Liquidity, volume and volatility characteristics.

9. Expected Academic Contributions

The study is expected to develop an analytical framework connecting technology-enabled trading, price discovery and cross-exchange price discrepancies. It may provide evidence on the importance of transaction costs, liquidity and execution constraints when evaluating arbitrage opportunities in Indian capital markets.

These are expected contributions, not reported research findings. Their validity will depend on the empirical results.

10. Practical Implications

  • Investors: Better understanding of price differences, liquidity and execution risk.
  • Market intermediaries: Insights into trading infrastructure and execution quality.
  • Regulators: Evidence that may inform discussions on market integrity and technology-enabled trading.
  • Researchers: A framework for further studies on FinTech and market efficiency.

11. Limitations

  • Availability of comparable, high-frequency NSE and BSE data may be limited.
  • Historical price differences may not reflect executable trading opportunities.
  • Transaction costs and market impact may vary across investors and time periods.
  • Reliable information on individual algorithmic orders and execution latency may not be publicly available.
  • Results may depend on sample selection and the chosen observation period.

12. Conclusion

FinTech has become an important influence on the structure and functioning of capital markets. Examining its relationship with market efficiency and arbitrage opportunities is relevant to the continuing development of Indian financial markets. By comparing selected securities across NSE and BSE, this proposed study aims to assess price discrepancies, liquidity, trading activity and the practical feasibility of arbitrage after transaction costs.

The final conclusions will be drawn only after systematic data collection, statistical analysis and interpretation of empirical results.
