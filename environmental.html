Environmental

The environmental dimension of ESG considers how an organisation affects and depends on the natural environment, including climate, resources, pollution and biodiversity.

1. Concept and meaning

The environmental dimension of ESG considers how an organisation affects and depends on the natural environment, including climate, resources, pollution and biodiversity.

Core idea: Environmental metrics should be comparable over time, transparently defined and supported by reliable data.

2. Key concepts and tools

Common indicators include greenhouse-gas emissions, energy and water use, waste generation and environmental incidents. Metrics need clear boundaries and units.

Use the relevant formula or analytical framework only when its assumptions fit the problem. State units, time period and assumptions clearly.

3. Worked example / practical illustration

A factory tracks electricity use per unit produced. A lower intensity may indicate efficiency improvement, but total emissions can still rise if production expands.

4. Classroom case study

Students conduct a simple environmental review of a campus building, recording energy, water, waste and improvement opportunities.

Discussion prompts

  • What information is missing before making a recommendation?
  • What are the financial and non-financial risks?
  • Which assumptions should be tested?

5. Learning activity

BBA: BBA: identify environmental impacts in a business.

MBA: MBA: evaluate materiality, emissions scopes, targets and transition risks.
