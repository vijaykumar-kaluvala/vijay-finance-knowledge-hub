1. Real-world learning cases

Case A: Digital lending

A small retailer applies for a working-capital loan through a digital platform. The platform uses electronic records to assess the application.

Discuss: How can digital lending improve access to credit? What risks arise from inaccurate data, privacy issues, hidden charges or unsuitable loans?

Learning point: Faster processing can improve access, but transparent pricing, responsible underwriting, data protection and a clear grievance process remain important.

Case B: Green bond

A company issues a bond to finance a solar-energy project. Investors receive interest, while the issuer reports how proceeds are used.

Discuss: What information should investors check before deciding whether the bond supports a credible green project?

Learning point: Review the use of proceeds, project selection, reporting, measurable environmental outcomes and the issuer's credit risk.

Case C: UPI payments

A small shop accepts digital payments instead of relying only on cash.

Discuss: How might this affect convenience, transaction records, customer service and cyber-fraud exposure?

Learning point: Digital payments can improve convenience and record-keeping, while users still need secure credentials, transaction checks and fraud awareness.

Case D: ESG screening

An investor compares two firms. One has strong profits but repeated pollution penalties; the other reports energy efficiency, worker safety and independent board oversight.

Discuss: Why might an investor examine ESG factors alongside financial statements?

Learning point: ESG factors can reveal risks and practices that financial figures alone may not fully explain. They do not guarantee investment performance.
