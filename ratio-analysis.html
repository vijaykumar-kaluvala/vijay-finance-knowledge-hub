Ratio Analysis

Ratio analysis converts financial-statement figures into relationships that help evaluate liquidity, profitability, efficiency and solvency.

1. Concept and meaning

Ratio analysis converts financial-statement figures into relationships that help evaluate liquidity, profitability, efficiency and solvency.

Core idea: No single ratio proves performance; compare trends, peer benchmarks, business model and accounting policies.

2. Key concepts and tools

Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities. Gross Profit Ratio = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100%. Debt-Equity Ratio = Debt / Equity. Interpret ratios with industry, time and accounting context.

Use the relevant formula or analytical framework only when its assumptions fit the problem. State units, time period and assumptions clearly.

3. Worked example / practical illustration

Current assets of ₹12 lakh and current liabilities of ₹6 lakh give a current ratio of 2:1.

4. Classroom case study

A retailer's sales rise while its inventory days and receivables days also rise. Students assess whether growth is tying up more working capital.

Discussion prompts

  • What information is missing before making a recommendation?
  • What are the financial and non-financial risks?
  • Which assumptions should be tested?

5. Learning activity

BBA: BBA: calculate and classify key ratios.

MBA: MBA: analyse DuPont relationships, peer comparisons and ratio limitations.

6. Check your understanding

2. Does a higher ratio always mean better performance?
3. Why compare ratios across periods?

7. Key takeaway

No single ratio proves performance; compare trends, peer benchmarks, business model and accounting policies.

Academic note: This is a learning resource, not investment advice. Verify current rules, market data and source documents before citing or making decisions.
