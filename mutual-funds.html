Mutual Funds

A mutual fund pools money from investors and invests according to a stated scheme objective and portfolio mandate.

1. Concept and meaning

A mutual fund pools money from investors and invests according to a stated scheme objective and portfolio mandate.

Core idea: Diversification can reduce company-specific risk but does not eliminate market risk; expenses and suitability matter.

2. Key concepts and tools

NAV per unit = (Market value of assets − Liabilities) / Units outstanding. Investor value = Units held × NAV, before applicable charges and taxes.

Use the relevant formula or analytical framework only when its assumptions fit the problem. State units, time period and assumptions clearly.

3. Worked example / practical illustration

If net assets are ₹100 crore and 10 crore units are outstanding, NAV is ₹10 per unit.

4. Classroom case study

Compare an index fund and an actively managed equity fund. Students should assess objective, benchmark, expenses, risk, portfolio and time horizon rather than relying only on past returns.

Discussion prompts

  • What information is missing before making a recommendation?
  • What are the financial and non-financial risks?
  • Which assumptions should be tested?

5. Learning activity

BBA: BBA: calculate NAV and compare broad fund categories.

MBA: MBA: evaluate benchmark, tracking error, expense ratio, portfolio risk and performance consistency.
