3. Understand the financial concepts

Net Present Value (NPV): Present value of expected cash inflows minus the initial investment. A positive NPV indicates that the project is expected to earn more than the required return.

NPV = Σ [Cash Flowt ÷ (1 + r)t] − Initial Investment

Internal Rate of Return (IRR): The discount rate at which the project's NPV becomes zero. Compare IRR with the required rate of return.

0 = Σ [Cash Flowt ÷ (1 + IRR)t] − Initial Investment

Payback Period: Time required for cumulative cash inflows to recover the initial investment. This simple measure does not consider the time value of money.

Learning note: This tool assumes annual end-of-year cash flows and a conventional project with one initial outflow followed by non-negative inflows. IRR may not be meaningful for unusual cash-flow patterns; use NPV as the primary value-based decision measure.
