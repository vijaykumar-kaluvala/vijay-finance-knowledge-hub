🌱 Green Finance & ESG

Study how financial decisions can support environmental sustainability, social responsibility and sound governance.

Back to Student Zone

Learning Notes

1. Meaning of green finance

Green finance directs financial resources toward activities that provide environmental benefits, such as renewable energy, energy efficiency, clean transport and sustainable water management.

2. ESG framework

Environmental factors include emissions, energy and resource use. Social factors include employee welfare, human rights and community impact. Governance includes board oversight, ethics, transparency and accountability.

3. Green bonds

Green bonds are debt instruments whose proceeds are earmarked for eligible green projects under the applicable framework. Investors should examine use-of-proceeds reporting, project selection and impact reporting.

4. Sustainable finance

Sustainable finance integrates environmental, social and governance considerations into financing and investment decisions. It includes green, social, sustainability and sustainability-linked instruments.

5. Climate finance

Climate finance supports mitigation, such as renewable energy and energy efficiency, and adaptation, such as climate-resilient infrastructure and water management.

6. ESG analysis

Investors may assess material ESG risks and opportunities alongside financial performance. ESG ratings can differ because providers use different data, weights and methodologies.

7. Greenwashing

Greenwashing occurs when environmental claims are misleading, exaggerated or unsupported. Check measurable targets, credible disclosures, independent verification and actual outcomes.

Practice Questions with Solutions

1. Case study: Solar project

A company proposes a solar installation that costs ₹10 lakh and expects annual electricity savings of ₹2 lakh for 6 years. What simple payback period does it have?

Solution / Suggested answer: Simple Payback Period = Initial Investment / Annual Cash Savings = ₹10,00,000 / ₹2,00,000 = 5 years. This ignores discounting and cash flows after payback.

2. Analysis question

A company publishes a net-zero target but gives no baseline, interim milestones or emissions data. Identify two concerns.

Solution / Suggested answer: The target is difficult to assess without a baseline and measurable interim milestones. Lack of transparent emissions data prevents stakeholders from evaluating progress and may create greenwashing risk.

3. Theory question

Differentiate green bonds and sustainability-linked bonds.

Solution / Suggested answer: Green bonds earmark proceeds for eligible green projects. Sustainability-linked bonds generally link financial or structural characteristics to achievement of specified sustainability performance targets; proceeds need not be restricted to green projects.
