📈 Financial Markets

Explore the institutions and instruments through which savings are mobilised and funds are transferred to users.

Learning Notes

1. Meaning and functions

A financial market facilitates the transfer of funds between savers and borrowers. It supports capital formation, liquidity, price discovery and risk transfer.

2. Money market and capital market

The money market deals mainly in short-term funds and instruments, generally up to one year. The capital market provides medium- and long-term finance through equity and debt instruments.

3. Primary and secondary markets

In the primary market, securities are issued to investors for the first time or through a further issue. In the secondary market, existing securities are traded among investors.

4. Equity and debt instruments

Equity represents ownership and may provide dividends and voting rights. Bonds and debentures represent borrowing and generally carry interest obligations.

5. IPO, FPO and OFS

An IPO is a company’s first public issue of shares. An FPO is a further public issue by an already listed company. In an Offer for Sale, existing shareholders offer shares for sale; proceeds generally go to selling shareholders.

6. Mutual funds and ETFs

Mutual funds pool investor money and invest according to a stated objective. ETFs are traded on exchanges and typically track an index or asset basket.

7. Regulation and investor awareness

SEBI regulates India’s securities market. Investors should read offer documents, understand risk disclosures, verify intermediaries and avoid guaranteed-return claims.

Practice Questions with Solutions

1. Problem 1: Bond interest

A bond with face value ₹10,000 carries a 7% annual coupon. Find annual coupon interest.

Solution / Suggested answer: Annual interest = ₹10,000 × 7% = ₹700.

2. Problem 2: Equity return

An investor buys a share for ₹200, receives a dividend of ₹8 and sells it for ₹220. Calculate holding-period return.

Solution / Suggested answer: Return = (Dividend + Sale price − Purchase price) / Purchase price × 100 = (8 + 220 − 200)/200 × 100 = 14%.

3. Theory question

Distinguish primary and secondary markets.

Solution / Suggested answer: Primary markets raise funds for issuers through new securities. Secondary markets provide liquidity by enabling investors to trade securities already issued.
