🏦 Banking & Investment

Learn how banks, investment institutions and investors manage funds, credit, returns and financial risks.

Learning Notes

1. Commercial banking

Banks accept deposits, provide loans and advances, facilitate payments and offer services such as remittances and guarantees.

2. Types of deposits

Savings accounts support regular savings and transactions. Current accounts are designed for frequent business transactions. Fixed deposits hold money for a specified period; recurring deposits accept periodic contributions.

3. Loans and credit appraisal

Credit appraisal examines repayment capacity, cash flows, credit history, collateral and the purpose of borrowing. Borrowers should compare interest rate, fees, tenure and total repayment.

4. Investment banking

Investment banks and merchant bankers assist with capital raising, public issues, underwriting, mergers and acquisitions and corporate financial advisory, subject to applicable regulation.

5. Risk and return

Common investment risks include market risk, credit risk, liquidity risk, interest-rate risk and inflation risk. Higher expected return is generally associated with higher risk, but higher returns are never guaranteed.

6. Diversification and asset allocation

Diversification spreads investments across assets or issuers to reduce concentration risk. Asset allocation sets the broad mix of equity, debt and other assets according to goals, horizon and risk tolerance.

7. Credit ratings

Credit ratings express an agency’s opinion about the relative credit risk of a debt instrument or issuer. They are not a guarantee of repayment and can change over time.

Practice Questions with Solutions

1. Problem 1: Simple interest

A person deposits ₹50,000 at 6% simple interest for 2 years. Find interest and maturity value.

Solution / Suggested answer: Interest = P × R × T / 100 = 50,000 × 6 × 2 / 100 = ₹6,000. Maturity value = ₹56,000.

2. Problem 2: EMI concept

A borrower takes a loan. What are the components of each standard EMI?

Solution / Suggested answer: An EMI consists of an interest component and a principal-repayment component. The interest portion is generally higher at the beginning of a standard reducing-balance loan and declines as principal is repaid.

3. Theory question

Why is diversification important?

Solution / Suggested answer: Diversification reduces dependence on the performance of a single security or asset. It can reduce unsystematic risk, although it cannot eliminate overall market risk.
