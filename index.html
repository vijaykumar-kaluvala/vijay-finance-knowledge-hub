📖 Finance Glossary — Quick Reference

Asset: A resource expected to provide future economic benefit.
Liability: An obligation that may require future settlement.
Equity: Ownership interest in a business.
Liquidity: Ability to meet short-term obligations.
Leverage: Use of fixed-cost financing or operating costs to influence returns and risk.
Dividend: Distribution of earnings to shareholders, subject to company policy and applicable rules.
IPO: Initial Public Offering of securities to public investors.
OFS: Offer for Sale, where existing shareholders sell shares.
NPV: Present value of expected cash inflows less initial investment.
WACC: Weighted average cost of the firm's sources of capital.
ESG: Environmental, Social and Governance factors.
FinTech: Technology applied to financial products and services.

FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT • UNIT 1

Introduction to Financial Management

Simple, classroom-friendly notes for understanding the foundations of financial management.

1. Meaning of Financial Management

Financial Management is the process of planning, obtaining, allocating and controlling financial resources so that an organization can achieve its objectives efficiently.

Simple idea: Finance management answers three practical questions: Where should money be invested? How should money be raised? How should profits/cash be managed?

2. Objectives of Financial Management

🎯 Wealth Maximization

Focuses on increasing the long-term value created for owners/shareholders, considering timing, risk and cash flows.

💧 Liquidity

Maintaining sufficient funds to meet obligations when they become due.

📈 Profitability

Using financial resources efficiently to support sustainable earnings.

⚖️ Risk–Return Balance

Evaluating expected returns together with the financial risks associated with decisions.

3. Profit Maximization vs Wealth Maximization

PointProfit MaximizationWealth Maximization
Main focusAccounting profitLong-term value
Time value of moneyMay not be fully reflectedExplicitly important
RiskMay receive less emphasisRisk is considered
Decision perspectiveShorter-term profit orientation can ariseLong-term value and cash-flow perspective

4. Functions of a Finance Manager

💼 Investment Decision

Decide where organizational funds should be invested, including long-term assets and projects.

🏦 Financing Decision

Decide the appropriate sources of funds, such as equity, debt and retained earnings.

💵 Dividend Decision

Determine how earnings may be distributed and how much may be retained, subject to the firm's needs and policy.

🔄 Working Capital Decision

Manage short-term assets and liabilities so the organization can operate smoothly while using funds efficiently.

5. Three Major Financial Decisions

DecisionKey QuestionExample
InvestmentWhere should we invest?Buy machinery or invest in a new project?
FinancingHow should we raise funds?Debt, equity or retained earnings?
DividendHow much earnings should be distributed?Pay dividend or retain funds for expansion?

6. Simple Real-World Example

ABC Ltd. has ₹10 crore available.

Management is considering a new manufacturing project costing ₹6 crore. It must decide whether the expected future cash flows justify the investment. It also needs to decide how to finance the project and whether any remaining earnings should be distributed or retained.

Students identify: Investment decision → ₹6 crore project   |   Financing decision → source of ₹6 crore   |   Dividend/retention decision → treatment of available earnings.

7. Scope of Financial Management

  • Financial planning and forecasting
  • Investment and capital budgeting decisions
  • Capital structure and financing decisions
  • Working capital management
  • Dividend and retention decisions
  • Financial analysis and control
  • Risk management and financial decision support
🎯 Classroom Activity — “You Are the Finance Manager”

Give each group a fictional company with ₹10 crore available. Ask students to allocate the money among expansion, working capital, debt repayment and retained funds. Each group must explain its decision using return, risk, liquidity and long-term value.

🧠 Unit 1 Quick Quiz

Which decision answers the question: “How should the company raise funds?”

📌 Unit 1 takeaway: Financial Management is about making sound decisions regarding investment, financing, dividends and working capital while balancing return, risk, liquidity and long-term value.

SUSTAINABLE FINANCE

ESG & Green Finance

A practical introduction to Environmental, Social and Governance factors and the financial instruments used to support sustainable economic activity.

🌍 E — Environmental

Climate change, greenhouse-gas emissions, energy use, renewable energy, water, waste, pollution and biodiversity.

👥 S — Social

Employee welfare, health and safety, human rights, diversity, community impact, customer responsibility and data/privacy issues.

🏛️ G — Governance

Board structure, ethics, transparency, shareholder rights, executive remuneration, audit, controls and anti-corruption practices.

💚 What is Green Finance?

Finance directed toward activities or projects that deliver environmental benefits, such as renewable energy, clean transport or energy efficiency.

🌱 Green Bonds

Debt instruments in which proceeds are intended for eligible environmental or climate-related projects, subject to the applicable framework and disclosure requirements.

💰 Sustainable Finance

A broader concept that considers environmental, social and governance factors in financing and investment decisions.

ESG vs Green Finance vs Sustainable Finance

ConceptSimple meaningExample
ESGFactors used to assess how an organization manages environmental, social and governance issues.Evaluating a company's emissions, worker safety and board practices.
Green FinanceFinancing connected specifically to environmental benefits.Funding a solar-energy project.
Sustainable FinanceBroader financing/investment approach considering sustainability factors, including environmental and social dimensions.Financing a project with environmental and social objectives.

Major Green Finance Instruments

Green Bonds

Debt raised for eligible green projects.

Green Loans

Loans whose use of proceeds is tied to eligible green activities under the relevant framework.

Sustainability-Linked Bonds

Debt whose financial or structural characteristics can be linked to predefined sustainability performance targets.

Sustainability-Linked Loans

Loan terms can be connected to agreed sustainability performance targets.

🎓 Classroom example
A company needs ₹100 crore to build a solar power project. If the financing is structured under an applicable green-finance framework and the project meets the relevant eligibility and reporting requirements, a green bond or green loan may be considered. Students can discuss: Who provides the money? What environmental benefit is expected? How should the use of proceeds be monitored?

Why ESG Matters in Finance

ESG information can be relevant to investors, lenders, companies and other stakeholders because environmental, social and governance issues may affect risks, opportunities, operating costs, access to capital and long-term business strategy. ESG analysis does not automatically mean that an investment is financially attractive; financial analysis and the quality of the underlying information remain important.

Simple ESG Evaluation Framework

Identify issue → Collect evidence → Measure relevant indicators → Assess risks/opportunities → Compare with objectives → Disclose/report → Monitor progress.

PRIMARY MARKET • IPO

Initial Public Offering (IPO)

A classroom-friendly guide to understand how a company raises money from the public and how investors participate.

1. What is an IPO?

An Initial Public Offering is the process through which a privately held company offers shares to public investors and becomes publicly listed.

2. Why does a company issue an IPO?

Common purposes include raising funds for expansion, debt reduction, working capital, acquisitions and other corporate needs.

3. Fresh Issue

New shares are issued by the company. The money raised goes to the company, subject to the offer documents and stated purpose.

4. OFS

In an Offer for Sale, existing shareholders sell their shares to the public. The sale proceeds generally go to those selling shareholders, not to the company.

5. Price Band

The offer document may specify a lower price (floor price) and upper price (cap price). Investors place bids within the permitted range.

6. Book Building

Investor bids at different prices help determine demand and contribute to the final issue price, subject to the issue structure and rules.

7. Application & Allotment

Investors submit applications during the issue period. If demand exceeds available shares, allocation follows the applicable category-wise rules and basis of allotment.

8. Listing

After allotment and completion of the issue process, the shares are listed on a stock exchange and can be traded in the secondary market.

Fresh Issue vs OFS

FeatureFresh IssueOFS
Who issues/sells?Company issues new sharesExisting shareholder(s) sell shares
Who generally receives the proceeds?CompanySelling shareholder(s)
New shares created?YesNo
Possible effect on existing ownershipMay dilute ownership because new shares are createdDepends on the seller and transaction; no new shares are created
🎓 Simple classroom example
Suppose the IPO price band is ₹258–₹272. An investor may place a bid within the permitted band, subject to the issue rules. If the final issue price is ₹272, an eligible investor allotted 100 shares would pay ₹27,200 before considering applicable charges/taxes.

IPO Journey — Easy Flow

Company decides to go public → appoints intermediaries → prepares offer documents → regulatory/issue process → IPO opens → investors bid → allotment → shares listed → secondary-market trading.
